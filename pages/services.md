## Three kinds of work

### Long-form storytelling (3–12 minutes)

Narrated films for YouTube, product pages and investor decks: a founder story, a product explainer in chapters, a documentary-style piece built from stills and motion, or an episodic series where one character has to stay recognisable across episodes. This is the format to pick when the argument needs room — a problem, a mechanism, a proof, a close.

You get a 1920×1080 master, an SRT caption file, and chapter markers if the runtime passes five minutes.

### 9:16 short-form ads (15–60 seconds)

Vertical cuts for Reels, TikTok and Shorts. The first three seconds carry a hook, the middle carries one claim, the last frame carries the action. Every cut is built to be watched with the sound off first, because that is how most of the feed will meet it, then mixed so it survives with the sound on.

Deliverables are 1080×1920 H.264 MP4 with burned-in or separate captions. When you need to test hooks, ask for two cuts from one concept instead of one polished cut — the cheaper test is usually the one that tells you which hook to scale.

### Custom brand song and sound design

An original track for the brand, not a stock loop: a 30–60 second theme, 15 and 30 second cutdowns for ads, and a short logo sting. Delivered as WAV and MP3, vocal and instrumental versions, with stems so your editor can remix later. Mixed to a streaming-safe master so the ad does not jump in volume between posts.

## How a project runs

1. **Brief.** You send the six items in the next section. If something is missing, you get one question list back, not a guess.
2. **Script and shot list.** Written first, in plain language, with cut count and duration per section. You approve or change it here, where changes are cheap.
3. **Stills.** Every shot is generated as a still and approved in sequence, against the audio. Faces, logos and horizons get fixed at this stage instead of after animation.
4. **Motion and assembly.** One camera move and one action per clip, cut to the beat or to the voiceover line.
5. **Delivery and one revision round.** You get the master, the caption file and a one-page note on what to change if you want to re-cut later.

## What to send with the brief

- [ ] The product or service in one sentence, and the one claim the video must land
- [ ] Platform and placement: feed, pre-roll, landing page, sales deck
- [ ] References: three links or frames you like, and one you do not
- [ ] Assets: logo files, product shots, brand colours and fonts, any footage you already own
- [ ] Music: your own track, a licensed track, or let the studio write one

## Rights, revisions and the limits

- **Usage.** Commercial use by the brand that commissioned the work is included. Reselling the film, licensing the same cut to another brand, or redistributing it as stock is not.
- **Revisions.** One round is included in the pilot. Larger projects get the number of rounds written into the quote, with free minor fixes (typos, caption position, timing nudges) separated from rebuilds (new concept, new music, redesigned character).
- **Music.** Your song stays yours. If the studio writes the track, the licence is written into the quote before work starts.
- **Real people.** No synthetic likeness of a real person without written consent, and no invented testimonial, review or performance claim placed inside the creative. If a script asks for a claim the product cannot support, it comes back as a question.
- **Platforms.** Files are delivered to spec; scheduling, ad accounts and media spend stay with you.
