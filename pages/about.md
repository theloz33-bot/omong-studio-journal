Omong Studio is one creator and a small stack of AI tools: an image model for stills, an image-to-video model for motion, a music generator for the soundtrack, a text-to-speech voice for narration, and an editor for the assembly. The work is vertical short-form series, music videos, and small self-contained apps that do one job without an account.

## What this journal is

Most writing about AI video is either a launch announcement or a warning. This is neither. It is the notebook: the order of operations that held a character together across nine episodes, the stage of a music-video pipeline that breaks when the mix is not locked, the reason a 90 KB HTML file beats a hosted app for a buyer who only wants the result.

Three rules apply to everything published here:

1. **Only workflows actually run.** If a technique has not been through a real project, it is not written up as advice.
2. **Failures stay in.** The stage that ate two days is more useful to a reader than the stage that worked first time.
3. **No inflated numbers.** No invented follower counts, no invented revenue, no "10x" claims. Where a figure appears, it is a workflow parameter — a shot count, a duration, a level in dB.

## What it covers

- **Character consistency** — keeping one face recognisable across a vertical episode series.
- **Music video** — locking audio first, then mapping and cutting picture against it.
- **Micro-tools** — shipping and selling single-file offline apps.

Posts are added as projects finish, not on a content calendar. There is an [RSS feed](https://theloz33-bot.github.io/omong-studio-journal/rss.xml) if you would rather read it that way.
